# Choose the faction-aware information model and tactics

Type: task
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 01

## Question

Given the recovered contract, what bounded, deterministic faction-aware scoring and truthfulness policy should the agent use for nominations, votes, legislative choices, and executive powers without risking an invalid response?

## Answer

Use a deterministic, visible-history-only `ReadModel` with two separate measures for every living seat: `fascist_score` (starts at 0) and `dictator_risk` (starts at 0). Never infer either from replay-only roles, deck order, model thoughts, or unobserved policy hands. Keep hard facts separately: own role; for a Fascist, the two team members and Dictator supplied at setup; investigation results; government membership; policy result; policy claims only when they are public statements; and nomination/vote history. `investigation-result=fascist` is a high Fascist score but does not reduce Dictator risk; `investigation-result=liberal` is a strong negative Fascist score and zero Dictator risk. A public liberal enactment makes both officials mildly safer, a public fascist enactment mildly more suspicious, and a claimed forced all-Fascist hand discounts (but never reverses) that suspicion. Use small fixed weights, cap scores, and exact seat-order tie breaks. Do not try to parse free-form claims beyond explicitly structured events.

For Liberal behavior, nominate and approve the eligible government with the lowest combined President/Chancellor score. Once three Fascist policies are enacted, make an uninvestigated Chancellor's Dictator risk a hard rejection unless every legal alternative is at least as risky; a known-Liberal investigation is the strongest admissible Chancellor evidence. At zero evidence, approve the first legal government rather than manufacture chaos. Before chaos, reject a government only when its combined risk passes a conservative threshold; at two failed governments, compare the proposed government against the unknown chaos policy and accept an otherwise borderline low-risk pair. Never approve a known Fascist investigator result as Chancellor at three Fascist policies. Investigate the highest `dictator_risk` eligible seat, execute the highest combined risk seat (favor confirmed Fascists), and call a special election for the lowest combined risk eligible President. These choices are always filtered by the protocol ticket's legal target set first.

For Fascist behavior, treat setup knowledge as authoritative. Nominate and vote for known Fascist governments while Fascist policy progress is below three, but do not expose the Dictator unnecessarily. At three or more Fascist policies, a Fascist President nominates the known Dictator whenever legal and a Fascist votes yes for a government with that Dictator as Chancellor; otherwise select the safest known teammate. A Fascist President uses investigation, special election, and execution to protect the Dictator and remove or bypass the strongest Liberal candidate, without targeting a known teammate. The Dictator, which lacks team knowledge in this profile, uses the Liberal-facing public score model and votes for any government that would elect itself Chancellor after three Fascist policies; it otherwise stays plausibly low-risk and does not act on nonexistent private team knowledge.

Legislation is direct and hand-validated: a Liberal President discards Fascist if available and a Liberal Chancellor enacts Liberal if available; a Fascist President discards Liberal if that forces or increases the chance of Fascist enactment, and a Fascist Chancellor enacts Fascist when present. A Fascist may choose a Liberal enactment only under an explicit, tested camouflage rule; omit that rule in the first version because it dilutes the policy objective and is not needed to beat the deterministic baseline. A Liberal Chancellor requests a veto only with two Fascists, five or more Fascist policies, and no prior rejection; a Fascist Chancellor accepts no Liberal-saving veto. The President accepts a Liberal veto request in the same terminal-risk case and otherwise follows the policy objective. Each policy selection is then validated against the private hand; if state evidence is incomplete, delegate to the legal fallback.

Keep `discuss` simple and bounded: pass by default; on a discussion immediately after a policy, emit at most one short, ASCII-only public line that states the policy result and, at three Fascist policies, warns against a high-`dictator_risk` Chancellor. Never expose private policy hands, setup knowledge, investigation result received privately, or internal scores. The strategy layer returns only intent (`target`, `vote`, `policy`, or pass); a final response builder rechecks response type, enum, and target/policy membership and substitutes the contract fallback on any mismatch or exception. Do not make an AI relay call in the critical decision path: the deterministic score model has bounded cost, latency, and output shape. This is a strong design hypothesis, not a measured win-rate claim.
